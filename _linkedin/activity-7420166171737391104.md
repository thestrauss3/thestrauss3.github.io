text: "Metrics are useful tools, but they’re easy to over-trust or misinterpret. Most metrics are narrow by nature. The real skill is knowing when a metric is lying by omission.\n\nI’ve seen this show up in a few places:\n• Retention interpreted as employee satisfaction, when it can also reflect external job market conditions\n• Sprint velocity interpreted as productivity, when it’s heavily influenced by the phase of work and context switching\n• Bug counts interpreted as code quality, when they’re often driven by usage and reporting. Rising counts can reflect better visibility or more customers using a feature, while declining counts can reflect reduced usage or weaker reporting.\n\nNone of these interpretations are unreasonable. They’re just incomplete without context. Metrics don’t become misleading because they’re wrong. They become misleading when we forget what they can’t see.\n\nWhat signals do you look for to validate—or challenge—the story a metric seems to tell?"
