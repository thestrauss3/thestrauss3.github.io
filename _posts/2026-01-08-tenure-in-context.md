Lately I’ve been thinking about how companies use metrics to understand performance, growth, and overall employee health.

Metrics are powerful tools, but they’re easy to misread when taken in isolation or without broader context.

One example that comes up frequently is employee tenure. Longer average tenure is frequently interpreted as a sign that people are happy and engaged. Sometimes that’s true. But it’s not the whole story.

From roughly 2019 through the early 2020s, the labor market shifted heavily in favor of employees. Remote work expanded, hiring accelerated, and job mobility increased. People moved more often because opportunities were plentiful and risk was lower.

Over the past couple of years, that dynamic has shifted back toward an employer-driven market. With tighter hiring, layoffs, and more competition for open roles, stability and financial security naturally become higher priorities.

In that context, rising tenure can reflect reduced external options as much as internal satisfaction.

It’s a reminder that people metrics don’t exist in a vacuum. Market conditions, opportunity cost, and risk tolerance all shape the signals we see.

Curious how others approach this:\\
What metrics or signals have you found most reliable when trying to distinguish retention from genuine engagement?
