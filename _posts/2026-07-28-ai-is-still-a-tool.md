For years, everyone around me was worried AI was going to replace software engineers.

I was usually the one arguing the opposite.

My take was that AI was going to fundamentally change what it means to be a developer, not eliminate the need for developers. I still believe that.

Then, after almost nine years with the same company, I got caught up in a layoff.

Nobody came out and said, "We're replacing engineers with AI." But it's hard to ignore what's happening across the industry. Teams are getting leaner, AI tools are getting better, and companies are expecting fewer engineers to do more work.

The irony isn't lost on me.

The thing is, I use AI every day. It's an incredible tool. It saves me time, catches things I miss, writes boilerplate, and lets me focus on harder problems.

But it's still a tool.

It doesn't know there's a design document unless someone points it there. It doesn't know a requirement changed because of a conversation that happened three months ago. It'll confidently invent an API that never existed if it's missing context.

People don't.

Good engineers ask questions. We notice when requirements contradict each other. We recognize when we're solving the wrong problem before we spend a week building the wrong solution. We understand the business behind the code, not just the code itself.

That's the part of software engineering I'm betting on.

So yes, I'm looking for my next opportunity.

If your team is using AI to make engineers more effective instead of treating AI like an engineer, I'd love to talk.
